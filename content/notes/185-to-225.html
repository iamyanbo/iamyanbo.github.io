The infamous 225

My experience training to hit 225.

Yanbo Cheng

185 lb

225 lb
The five-week target

For about two years, I went to the gym six days a week. I was consistent, but for the most part I was going through the motions. I wasn’t pushing myself as much as I could, and my bench one-rep max was 185 pounds.

Then I decided I wanted to bench 225, and I thought five weeks was a good timeline.

Smolov Jr.

I came across Smolov Jr., a three-week program often used to improve your bench PR. I had read about people adding 20–30 pounds to their max. I figured I could extend it to five weeks and aim for 40.

Forty pounds in five weeks was a lot to ask, especially considering fatigue. But I felt that if I locked in, ate enough, and focused on training, I could do it. I didn’t really have the mentality that I wasn’t going to hit it.

My schedule

I trained chest four days a week. I skipped legs for these five weeks because I wanted to focus on bench, but kept back in for stability.

I wanted to put everything into these sessions. Not 100%, but 110%.

I started at 145 pounds on the first bench day, with heavier weights on the other three days. Each week, I added 10 pounds to all four sessions.

I flew through the first two weeks because the weights felt light. That built up my confidence for the coming weeks.

Food

This part was fun. I basically got to munch on whatever I wanted and didn’t care about the calories. I still focused on getting enough protein to grow, but I really enjoyed eating more.

Exceeding expectations

My bench exploded. Near the end of the five weeks, I was feeling good and decided to try 225. I hit it three days early.

I’d spent two years being consistent without pushing myself enough. For these five weeks, I had something I really wanted, and I put everything into it. It was also just a lot of fun.
