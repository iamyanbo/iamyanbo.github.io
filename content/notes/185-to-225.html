From 185 to 225.

Two years of showing up, then a five-week goal that changed how I trained.

Yanbo Cheng

225 lb
The five-week target
3 days
Ahead of my deadline

For about two years, I went to the gym six days a week. I was consistent, but a lot of the time I was going through the motions. I wasn’t pushing myself as much as I could, and my bench one-rep max was 185 pounds.

Then I decided I wanted to bench 225. I gave myself five weeks.

A goal I could put a date on

I came across Smolov Jr., a three-week program often used for bench press. I had read about people adding 20–30 pounds to their max, and I started thinking about extending the idea to five weeks and aiming for 40.

That was an aggressive target. But I felt that if I focused on eating and training for those five weeks, I could hit it. I didn’t go into the block with the mentality that I might not get there. I was thinking about what I needed to do to make it happen.

The training block

For this block, I trained chest four days a week. I skipped legs temporarily to focus on the bench goal, but kept two back days because I wanted that work to support my stability on the bench.

The effort I put into those sessions felt completely different from the previous two years. In my head, it was “110%”: I had a specific number I was working toward, and I was taking each session seriously.

I started the block at 145 pounds and planned to add 10 pounds to the working weights each week. The four bench sessions followed this set-and-rep pattern:

The four sessions above describe my five-week adaptation. The linked Smolov Jr. reference covers the standard three-week program.

I flew through the first two weeks because the weights felt light. Getting through those sessions so comfortably built my confidence for the coming weeks, when the weights would be heavier.

I enjoyed the eating part, too

One of the fun parts was getting to eat more. I focused on protein, and food became part of the goal instead of something separate from training. For that block, I enjoyed being able to put so much attention into eating and lifting.

Three days early

My bench started moving quickly. Near the end of the block, I felt good enough to try 225 before the deadline. I got it three days early.

I had set out to add 40 pounds to my bench in five weeks, and I had done it with time to spare. That felt very different from simply finishing another week of gym sessions.

What I took from it

I don’t think the two years before this were wasted. I already had the habit of showing up six days a week. The five-week goal gave that habit a direction, and I put more effort into the training and food around it.

I also had a lot of fun doing it. There was a number I cared about, a date I wanted to hit it by, and something specific to work on every time I went in.

225 went up three days early. I’m still very happy about that.
