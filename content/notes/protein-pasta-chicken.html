Protein

For muscle growth, I like the simple rule of 1 g of protein per pound of body weight per day. If you weigh 160 lb, that’s 160 g. Easy to remember.

You can eat less and still build muscle. For context, ISSN’s guidance puts most exercising people at about 1.4–2.0 g/kg a day, or 0.64–0.91 g/lb. So 1 g/lb is a convenient target, rather than a requirement. Around 0.7 g/lb sits within that range; 0.6 g/lb is a little below it.

Macros, quickly

Protein, carbs, and fat are the three macros. For rough calorie maths, protein and carbs each give about 4 kcal per gram; fat gives about 9. That’s why the fat percentage on beef makes such a difference. Source ↗

Daily calories

As a rough reference, the NHS gives about 2,000 kcal a day for an average woman and 2,500 for an average man. Your age, body size, and how active you are all affect how much you need.

Don’t forget the sauce

I can get away with pretty bland food because I like zero-calorie sauces. But sauces can vary massively in calories. A bit of mustard and a big pour of mayo are very different additions.

Generic sauces · portions weighed in grams
Sauce15 g45 g
Yellow mustard9 kcal27 kcal
Ketchup15 kcal45 kcal
Barbecue sauce26 kcal77 kcal
Regular mayonnaise102 kcal306 kcal

Those are weighed portions, not “one squeeze” or “a spoonful.” For something labelled zero-calorie, I still check the serving size on the bottle. And cooking oil counts too: 10 g of olive oil adds about 88 kcal.

I don’t need every meal to be exciting. Pasta, potatoes, chicken, enough protein, and a sauce I like works for me.
